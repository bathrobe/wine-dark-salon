Ong coined "secondary orality" for TV/radio—literate people performing oral traits like group-mindedness and spontaneity. This reversed print's detachment but kept audiences passive. Logan's "tertiary orality" added the missing piece: interactive texting turned viewers into participants.

> According to Ong, "Secondary orality is both remarkably like and remarkably unlike primary orality." The resemblance to primary orality lay "in its participatory mystique, its fostering of a communal sense, its concentration on the present moment, and even its use of formulas."
> — Walter Ong
