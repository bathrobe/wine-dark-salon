Medieval scholars used Latin as a second language for all abstract thinking. This separation from mother-tongue emotion created the objectivity needed for modern science. Newton wrote and thought in Latin, not English.

> Learned Latin effects even greater objectivity by establishing knowledge in a medium insulated from the emotion-charged depths of one's mother tongue, thus reducing interference from the human lifeworld and making possible the exquisitely abstract world of medieval scholasticism
> — Walter J. Ong
