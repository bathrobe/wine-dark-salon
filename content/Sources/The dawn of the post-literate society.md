# The Dawn of the Post-Literate Society - Summary & Analysis

**Source:** [James Marriott - Cultural Capital](https://substack.com/@jamesmarriott716869/p-173338158)  
**Date:** September 19, 2025  
**Clipped:** November 10, 2025  
**Tags:** #literacy #technology #democracy #culture #education

## 12-Point Summary & Analysis

1. **The Reading Revolution (1700s):** An unprecedented democratization of knowledge where literacy exploded across social classes, fundamentally reshaping European society and birthing the Enlightenment, democracy, and modern science.

2. **The Counter-Revolution (2010s):** Smartphone adoption marks a watershed moment reversing 300 years of literacy gains, with reading for pleasure down 40% in America and universities teaching their first "post-literate" cohorts.

3. **Cognitive Collapse:** Beyond declining literacy, we're seeing measurable drops in attention span, analytical thinking, and even IQ scores - the "Flynn effect" has reversed since the mid-2010s.

4. **The Golden Thread Breaking:** Like the collapse of Rome's literate culture, we're witnessing the second major rupture in humanity's transmission of knowledge, with classics becoming inaccessible to new generations.

5. **Democracy's Dependency:** Print culture's logical, rational discourse enabled democratic governance; screen culture's emotional, fragmented communication favors populism and authoritarianism.

6. **The Creativity Crisis:** From pop songs getting shorter to declining "disruptive" inventions, our culture shows signs of stagnation as deep reading - the foundation of innovation - disappears.

7. **Historical Irony:** The same moral panics that greeted the reading revolution ("reading excites the imagination too much") now seem prescient - literacy did indeed help destroy feudal hierarchies.

8. **The Attention Economy:** Where books demanded sustained focus, smartphones are designed for addiction, with Gen Z spending 9 hours daily on screens - equivalent to 25 years of waking life.

9. **Academic Breakdown:** Universities report students unable to read Dickens or follow complex arguments, fundamentally breaking the transmission of human knowledge across generations.

10. **The Nostalgia Question:** While this feels like classic "kids these days" anxiety, the scale and measurability of the decline (PISA scores, IQ trends) suggest this crisis differs from previous moral panics.

11. **Digital Literacy vs. Print Literacy:** Rather than simply returning to books, we may need new forms of digital literacy - but the article argues complex thinking literally requires the cognitive tools developed through sustained reading.

12. **The Feudal Future:** Without print-based critical thinking, citizens become as vulnerable to emotional manipulation as medieval peasants, potentially ushering in a new dark age of concentrated power and ignorance.

## Key Quote

> "If the reading revolution represented the greatest transfer of knowledge to ordinary men and women in history, the screen revolution represents the greatest theft of knowledge from ordinary people in history."
